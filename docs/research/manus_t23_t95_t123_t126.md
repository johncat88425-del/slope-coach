## T95 — English submission-page copy

**Submission copy (under 300 words):**

> **Slope Coach: Check motion against an explicit physics assumption**A short clip can make motion look clear while leaving important conditions uncertain. Slope Coach examines a fixed-camera video of a moving object against an explicitly selected model, such as free flight. The user confirms the time window and shooting conditions. Deterministic physics code computes the measurements and returns “consistent,” “inconsistent,” or “insufficient data,” with reasons and a track overlay for human review. The tool does not label a clip’s source.In the product-results run, two self-filmed clips were consistent, with apparent gravity values of 9.62 and 9.81 m/s². One Meta-source clip was inconsistent, with apparent gravity of 1.71 m/s² and a direction offset of −39° from image-down. Another returned insufficient data because apparent object size changed by 79% along the track, a possible perspective effect. These examples are exploratory and do not establish general reliability.Muse Spark is intended to ask clarifying questions and explain findings; SAM 3.1 is intended to track the object. Those integrations remain TBD until merged and tested. The current workflow may use a color-and-motion tracker and a fixed-template explanation. Measurements are generated by the program, not by a language model.Limits include fixed-camera and near-side-view assumptions, perspective, tracking quality, user-provided object size and a human-confirmed time window.

## T125 — User-test plan (replaces T22)

**Purpose:** Observe whether people can record and upload a suitable toss clip, understand the result and its limits, and identify where they get stuck. This is formative usability work, not a validation study.

**Participants and setup:** Recruit five people who can use their own phone camera. Use a test instance and a ball with a known diameter. Ask participants to avoid faces, personal documents, addresses and other identifying material in the frame. Explain what will be recorded, obtain consent before starting, and agree whether the clip may be retained; delete it after the session if retention was not explicitly agreed.

**Recording guidance:** Place the phone on a stable support for a side view. Keep one clearly visible ball and its full flight in frame, use normal-speed recording, and keep the ball moving across the image rather than toward or away from the camera. Use a clear, uncluttered background and a safe area. Do not ask participants to throw near people, breakable objects or traffic. If the capture does not meet the guidance, let the participant decide whether to retake it.

**Session script:**

1. Read the invitation and consent statement. Ask the participant to describe what they think the tool checks, without giving them the answer.

1. Ask them to record a ball toss with their phone and upload it to the test instance.

1. Ask them to complete the audit setup using their own understanding of the prompts. Do not coach unless they are blocked; if help is needed, note the exact prompt and intervention.

1. Ask them to explain the displayed verdict in their own words, name the evidence they used, and say what the tool does not conclude.

1. If the result is insufficient data, ask what they think caused it and what they would try next. Do not imply that refusal is an error.

1. Ask what was confusing, reassuring or missing, and whether they would change how they record the next clip.

**Recruitment message (English):**

> Hi! I’m testing an early prototype that checks whether a ball’s motion in a phone video is consistent with a selected physics assumption. I’m looking for a few people to record a short ball toss on their own phone, upload it to a test page, and tell me what the result means to them. This is a usability test, not a test of your physics knowledge. Please use a clear area, keep faces and personal information out of the frame, and do not include anything you would not want shared with the project team. You can stop at any time. The prototype may return “insufficient data”; that is useful feedback, not a mistake by you. Let me know if you’d like to take part.

## T126 — English limitations and FAQ

### What does Slope Coach tell me?

It checks whether the tracked motion is consistent or inconsistent with the physics model you selected, or whether there is insufficient data to make that comparison. The result is about the stated assumption and the available evidence.

### Why doesn’t it label a clip’s source or authenticity?

The tool measures motion against a physics assumption. Its evidence does not establish where a clip came from, so it does not assign a source or authenticity label. A motion result must not be treated as proof of origin.

### Why might I see “insufficient data”?

The track, timing, scale or camera view may not support a reliable comparison. For example, if apparent object size changes because the object moves toward or away from the camera, this two-dimensional check cannot resolve that perspective effect. The correct response is to withhold a conclusion rather than guess.

### How should I record a clip?

Use a stable phone position and a side view. Keep one clearly visible object and its full flight in frame, record at normal speed, and avoid having it move toward or away from the camera. Provide the object size and confirm the time window when asked. Keep the area safe and the background uncluttered.

### Where do the measurements come from?

The program’s physics code computes measurement values from the tracked points and the user-confirmed inputs. A language model must not invent or change those values. The interface should identify whether a track came from SAM 3.1, a color-and-motion fallback or another documented source.

### What do the Meta models do?

Muse Spark is intended to ask clarifying questions and explain computed findings; SAM 3.1 is intended to track the object. These steps are not to be described as live unless they have been merged and tested in the build being shown. If a fixed-question mock or template explanation is used, the interface must say so. The models do not supply the physics measurements.

### What are the current limitations?

The workflow assumes a suitable fixed-camera, near-side view of a single visible object. Perspective changes, motion blur, an unreliable track, uncertain object size or an unconfirmed time window can limit the result. The current evidence is exploratory and does not establish general reliability.
